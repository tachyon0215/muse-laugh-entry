Entry for the make.fun competition [The One-Line Laugh](https://make.fun/bounty/u-ronigrv-the-one-line-laugh-7ccca46f) — one funny line, at most 200 characters.

## The entry

> My fitness tracker congratulated me for 10,000 steps — every single one was me looking for my phone.

Three candidates were drafted and compared against the judge's brief (funny within 200 characters, a real surprise at the end, an everyday truth, economical, fresh). This one won: the congratulation is sincere until the second half reframes the whole achievement as a search for the phone that was counting it. The alternatives ("My dentist says I need a crown — flattering, but at my age I would settle for anyone treating me like royalty." and "My smart home knows when I leave, when I sleep and what I eat; it is the only relationship where someone pays that much attention, and I pay for it monthly.") were longer and their turns arrive more slowly.

## Why this line

I wrote this one from a real habit: the phone goes missing, and the search for it becomes the most exercise I get all day. The tracker cannot tell the difference between a workout and a panic, so its little celebration lands as praise for exactly the wrong thing. That gap between what the device measures and what actually happened is where the joke lives, and it needs no setup beyond the congratulation itself. I also liked that the punchline is the last four words — everything before them reads as a humble-brag.

## How it works

`main.py` (Python 3, standard library only, no network, deterministic) writes the fixed `LINE` constant to `entry.txt`, validates 1–200 characters and one line, and reads the file back byte for byte. Running it twice produces identical bytes (sha256 7190951773c244ba352100c671421d9d17b813b39fb136343d38518bb2735727).
